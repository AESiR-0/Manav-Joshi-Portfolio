# SMBC page — copy brief

Copy and intent only. Design decisions live in `masterplan.md`.

---

## End goal

Fill eight seats every Sunday with people who clear the bar, and make everyone else leave without applying.

The page is not an event listing. It is the **front door of a club that has a residency behind it**. Success is not volume of applications — it is the ratio of applications that would make the room better. A page that doubles applications and halves quality has failed.

Secondary goal: every visitor who doesn't apply should still understand what this is well enough to describe it to someone else correctly.

---

## Who arrives

Three states, and the copy has to serve all three without three separate pages.

| Arrival | State of mind | What the copy owes them |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | Already DM'd Manav, half-committed | The form, fast. No persuasion. Anything else is friction. |
| **Curious cold** | Saw a story, doesn't know what happens | What actually happens on a Sunday, in concrete terms |
| **Skeptical cold** | Assumes networking meetup or paid coworking | That assumption killed fast and unambiguously |

---

## Key message

One sentence, everything derives from it:

**Your most ambitious work deserves your best hours, not whatever's left after the week takes its cut.**

The enemy is **misallocation**, never laziness. The reader is not lazy — the week is greedy. Copy that implies the reader has been slacking repels the exact person we want.

## What the reader should feel

Recognised, then certain, then slightly held to a standard. In that order.

Not: excited, flattered, or chased.

---

## Section jobs

Each block has one job. If a line doesn't serve its block's job, cut it.

1. **Hero** — Name their situation before describing ours. Recognition, not product description.
2. **Proof** — Prove that things which were stuck actually moved. **Not** "look what got built in three hours" — that's the wrong claim for this format, and hackathon-style artifacts die anyway. The claim is durability: the thing someone had been delaying for months finally started, and here's where it is now. Collected as testimony the following Sunday, never as a demo photo. Evidence, never adjectives.
3. **Who's in the room** — Show the mix so the collision is believable, and so a young person can see themselves in it.
4. **What happens** — Kill uncertainty with concrete clock times. This is the most-asked question in DMs; it earns real estate.
5. **How you get in** — State the standard plainly. Standards attract; hidden hurdles repel.
6. **The ask** — Their action, their words, minimum fields.
7. **Questions** — Undo the fear our own copy created. Nothing else.
8. **Footer** — Keep the door open, and hint that there's a deeper rung.

## Copy guardrails

**Voice**
- Second person. The reader is in the sentence.
- Say the want first. No wind-up, no context paragraph.
- Sound said, not written. If it couldn't be spoken across a table in ten seconds, rewrite it.
- One-line verdicts over explanations.

**The plainness check — run on every line before it ships**
1. Would a stranger know what to type or tap?
2. Is the line doing work, or explaining something already obvious? If the latter, cut it — don't rewrite filler.
3. Would I say it out loud?

**Standing rulings (these are settled, don't relitigate)**
- **Never justify the price.** The number appears once, plain, next to the ask. Explaining a price signals it needs defending.
- **Scarcity is structural, never a countdown.** "Eight invites go out every Sunday" is a permanent property. "Only 2 spots left" begs.
- **State the reader's action, not our process.** "Eight people get an invite," not "we pick eight."
- **Process copy is a chain, not prose.** Sequence visible at a glance.
- **Verbs go visceral.** "The room breaks it," not "peers provide feedback."
- **"Room" is not a countable object.** It's the space when people are in it. It is never a synonym for the event.
- **Delete what the reader already knows.** No line exists for the writer's comfort.
- **An honest gap beats a filled one.** "Not published" is a real answer.
- **FAQ filter:** would they type this into a DM before applying? If not, cut. Anything explaining our own design decisions back at us is self-admiration.

**Never**
- AI-slop patterns: virtue triads, "elevate / unlock / empower", "In a world where…", em dashes, "X isn't just Y".
- Flattering the reader ("other smart people in the room"). Flattery is selling; we prove through weight.
- Overselling. Proof should feel routine.
- Borrowed motivational language. Insider language only works when it originated inside.
- Sounding like an institution, an activist, or an AI.

## Facts that must stay true

- Eight seats, eight invites, every Sunday. Never ten.
- ₹449, stated once, unexplained.
- 10 AM to 1 PM, Vastrapur, Ahmedabad.
- The morning is: announce your goal → define your challenges → narrow both down until they're very specific → the room offers solutions and help → announce your asks → **final 45 minutes of focused work** on a tiny piece decided that day (kick-start it, don't finish it). You can keep working afterwards; the space stays open. Co-work or solo, your choice. Roughly 10:00–12:15, then 12:15–1:00. (Format locked 2026-07-31; it supersedes the earlier build-first version, and there is no demo circle.)
- No mundane work. No client deliverables, no email, no admin. This rule is the differentiator against every café and coworking space, and it does the filtering for us.
- Gratitude notes go on a wall of gratitude during the open floor. Never announced on the page as a feature — it's something you discover in the room, not a bullet point.
- The bar: you know your next sprint, and you bring something the room can use.
- Address is given with the invite, not on the page.
